# References and priority review

The problem asks for distinct distances from a common vertex of a strictly convex polygon. The classical bounds are \(\lceil n/3\rceil\) from Moser, \(\lfloor n/3\rfloor+1\) from Erdős and Fishburn, \(\lceil(13n-6)/36\rceil\) from Dumitrescu, and \((13/36+1/22701)n-O(1)\) from Nivasch, Pach, Pinchasi and Zerbib. The last argument counts good edges and witnesses using minimum enclosing caps. Altman's all-pairs result addresses a different invariant. [Erdős original paper](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1946-03.pdf) · [Official problem and bibliography](https://www.erdosproblems.com/982) · [Moser](https://doi.org/10.1080/00029890.1952.11988075) · [Erdős–Fishburn](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02573998) · [Nivasch et al., full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1207.1266)

Kominers's author manuscript of 25 July 2026 obtains \(13/36+3/5270\) using cap imbalance and matching between successive enclosing-disk supports. The repository's \(13/36+1/1667\) gives a coefficient gain of exactly \(269/8785090\) over that preprint. The step specific to this improvement is allowing overlapping potential windows while charging deleted witnesses by their distance from the window edge. No stronger directly comparable public coefficient was identified in the reviewed sources. [Kominers manuscript, Theorems 1 and 18](https://scottkom.com/assets/articles/Kominers_Distinct_Distances_from_a_Vertex.pdf)

Fishburn's chapter predates the recent octagon proofs, as Lee's correction of 5 November records. Lee's 2 November public proof covers all \(3\le n\le13\); its repository also records small-\(n\) results for the *sum* variant and separate window properties. Quantavane formalized \(3\le n\le13\) independently of the DIMACS construction in this repository. The upstream proof uses native evaluation for SAT, with geometric lemmas in mathlib. Saller's note contains a separate finite encoding and small-case controls, including a staggered two-triangle example with pin counts 2 and 3. These sources give prior art for the small cases and exact overlap with the \(s=1\) example of 982-X04. The local \(14\le n\le19\) and 21 claims are beyond the explicit ranges of these three finite proofs, subject to broader priority review. [Fishburn chapter](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4614-7258-2_30) · [Lee fixed revision](https://github.com/Paulee314/erdos982/blob/ab7aa80494ceacbd6e3e278fb6f421d9c3c90d97/README.md) · [Quantavane source](https://github.com/arhancanli/quantavane-lean/tree/76a54a6c728161e75e1b0275c0b32ffc311f1f6a/erdos982-le13) · [Saller fixed revision](https://github.com/sallerk/erdos-notes/blob/91416d132a251a9eb76dc2a2e8cf58ab05829d53/p982/NOTE.md) · [Formal Conjectures statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/9097e2f90e66d2f6244209cb1c07f7120d17ec53/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/982.lean)

The 24 November curvature manuscript proves the \(n-1-O_d(n^{5/7})\) bound for one nonlinear polynomial parameter curve and for a nonsingular noncircular real conic. Its accompanying conic manuscript also overlaps the geometric all-size conic result. The several-graph theorem in this repository treats three possibly different graph polynomials in the incidence surface, assumes a common orthonormal coordinate system, and has explicit dependence on their count. Solymosi–Zahl's estimate applies only after irreducibility, the cylindrical exception, and the local additive exception have been dealt with. The rational-coordinate bound rests on a separating tree plus a pair count, with the bisector bound requiring no three collinear. The related all-pairs collinear-triple-free theorem in arXiv:2611.25148 uses a different invariant and a smaller bound. The search found no equivalent to the exact rational or ramified-field pinned bounds. [Curvature manuscript](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/8fda4f297987458e0402ce0a2382caad96f01b70/research/erdos-982-pinned-distances/CURVATURE.md) · [Conic manuscript](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/8fda4f297987458e0402ce0a2382caad96f01b70/research/erdos-982-pinned-distances/README.md) · [Solymosi–Zahl, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13294) · [Raz–Sharir–de Zeeuw](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05012) · [All-pairs refinement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25148)

Beukers–Smyth gives a bound of 22 times Newton polygon area for *finite* cyclotomic intersections of a complex Laurent polynomial. Its infinite case supplies a torsion binomial factor; the circle and elliptic proof drafts using it must exclude that case separately. The finite-generated-group majority approach also invokes the Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt theorem and a geometric classification of degenerate subsums. The rank and rigidity materials use block design-matrix ranks, with the Jacobian evaluated at the actual point configuration, and an affine Bézout bound for isolated solutions after freezing coordinates. These dependencies do not by themselves imply the project's thresholds. [Beukers–Smyth §4.1](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~chris/preprints/beukers_smyth.pdf) · [Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/155-3/p04) · [Dvir–Garg–Oliveira–Solymosi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08923) · [Heintz](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304397583900026) · [Szemerédi–Trotter](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/38.pdf)

The literature on centrally symmetric convex position supplies a relevant ambient class. The constant-width results concern finite pinned multiplicity and counts of qualifying pins. The literature on unimodal convex polygons already supplies the notion of unimodality. For cocircular rigidity, the older classifications of polygons minimizing *all-pairs* distances and Fishburn's sum conjectures give close comparisons. An equality assumption at every pin needs its own argument since the distance sets need not coincide from pin to pin. The two concentric 21-gons in the discussion of #1082 furnish a control when convexity is removed. ## Search coverage

The first pass on 26 November 2026 used the official problem and forum endpoints, original papers and author-hosted manuscripts, arXiv, and fixed GitHub revisions. Search concepts included equivalent pinned and single-vertex terminology, all-pairs versus pinned distances, rational and integral coordinates, Gaussian-integer parity, dyadic ramification, convexity and circle equality, constant width, concentric rings, and rank of isosceles constraints. Several browsing endpoints had access errors. Official problem HTML and the named GitHub source files were recovered separately. Further claim-specific priority work remains for unpublished geometric thresholds, uniform constants, and results linked only from private source notes. “Candidate contribution” in the register refers to the audited search, without a priority guarantee.
